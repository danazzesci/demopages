01 / The condition

A decision is forming before the evidence is complete.

Capital is more selective. Digital systems represent more of the organization’s operating reality. AI infrastructure is changing energy, computing, data, labor, and vendor economics.1 Delay is not neutral—but neither is speed.

01

Technology activity is not decision visibility.

More tools, data, and automation can coexist with an inability to explain what condition actually exists.

02

Compliance is necessary. It is not assurance.

A compliant organization can still accept an untested assumption, misallocate capital, or fail to obtain the outcome leadership expects.3

03

Commitment changes what remains reversible.

Architecture, vendors, workforce, operating process, and governance can harden into dependencies before expected value is visible.

02 / The implication

The technology is not the final decision.

Leadership must decide what organizational condition is supposed to change, which assumptions carry the case, and what evidence would justify continued commitment.

Observable

Capital and operations are moving toward a technology commitment.

Management meaning

The consequences extend beyond the system being acquired.

Technology consequence

New dependencies and constraints will persist after approval.

Decision requirement

Leadership must distinguish necessity, timing, and reversibility.

Evidence needed

A measurable state shift, tested assumptions, and accountable ownership.

The decision question

What must be true for this commitment to remain defensible after enthusiasm, budget, and organizational motion have been energized?

03 / Decision questions

What leadership needs to know before acting.

These are not generic discovery questions. They expose the conditions carrying the decision—and the uncertainty that cannot responsibly remain hidden.

Use this as a live test

If the answers are unclear, the decision may not yet be decision-ready.

What organizational condition is this investment intended to change?

Name the observable present state and the target state. “Adopt AI,” “modernize,” or “improve security” describes activity, not the condition that must change.

Which assumptions must remain true for the projected benefit to occur?

Separate validated facts from estimates about adoption, data quality, integration, workforce capacity, vendor performance, operating cost, and time to value.

What becomes harder to reverse after commitment?

Identify fixed costs, contractual constraints, data movement, architecture coupling, specialized skills, process changes, and accountability transferred to third parties.

What evidence would demonstrate that the intended state shift occurred?

Define evidence before approval: baseline, observable measure, decision owner, review date, acceptable variance, and the action triggered if results differ.

Which uncertainty must be reduced—and which can be retained and monitored?

Not all uncertainty can be removed. The task is to isolate what could materially change the choice, the sequence, the scale, or the ability to recover.

What is the cost of waiting compared with the cost of being wrong?

Compare option value, competitive position, operational exposure, financing, standards, talent, and supplier change against premature dependency and misallocated capital.

04 / Evidence discipline

Confidence should be traceable.

A defensible decision distinguishes what is observed from what is inferred—and what professional judgment recommends after the remaining uncertainty is made visible.

What leadership receives

A claim is decomposed.

  • Observable condition — what can be directly supported
  • Interpretation — what the evidence reasonably suggests
  • Decision implication — what leadership must now consider
  • Professional judgment — what action appears justified

What leadership can conclude

Proceed. Narrow. Sequence. Correct. Delay. Stop.

  • The choice is linked to evidence, not momentum
  • Dependencies and residual uncertainty are explicit
  • Ownership and review conditions are assigned
  • The rationale can be explained to accountable parties

06 / When judgment enters

Bring the decision into focus.

Independent judgment is most useful while the choice can still change—not after unresolved conditions have become sunk cost, delay, or operating dependency.

01

Before a major technology commitment

Clarify the condition, test the investment logic, identify irreversible commitments, and define the evidence required for approval.

↗
02

When leadership cannot reconcile competing claims

Separate technical fact, advocacy, assumption, and uncertainty so accountable leaders can determine what can reasonably be concluded.

↗
03

When an initiative moves from concept into operation

Test whether people, process, architecture, authority, and measurement can support the promised outcome in real conditions.

↗
04

When confidence exceeds the available evidence

Establish a baseline, validation criteria, review points, and the action leadership will take when results differ from expectation.

01 / The distinction

Knowing the technology does not answer the operational question.

Objectives and technical literacy matter. Leaders must also decide scope, discovery, operational modeling, uncertainty, validation, human authority, cost, and evidence.

Understanding AI

How it works.

  • LLMs and retrieval-augmented generation
  • Agents, orchestration, and tool calls
  • Hallucination and available products

Operational readiness

When we can rely on it.

  • Scope, observability, and exclusions
  • Relationships, evidence, provenance, and freshness
  • Validation, human authority, usefulness, and cost
The challenge is not merely learning how AI works. The challenge is constructing the conditions under which an organization can rely on it.

Before AI can reason about the estate, the organization must decide what estate it is responsible for knowing.

02 / The decisions

Twelve decisions stand between interest and dependable use.

Each must be recognized, assigned, funded, bounded, and validated.

Phase 01 · 01–02

Readiness

What is in scope, and how will we know what exists?

01

The enterprise placed within AI scope

Define the systems, identities, applications, services, data, processes, interfaces, and environments the capability must understand—and what remains outside responsibility.

02

The means of continuous discovery

Treat inventories, diagrams, cloud records, CMDB entries, and manifests as declarations requiring validation, not unquestioned ground truth.

Discovery produces observations. It does not automatically produce understanding.

Phase 02 · 03–06

Structure establishment

What does the estate look like, and how certain are we?

03

Operational relationships

Represent assets, identities, permissions, services, interfaces, data flows, trust paths, and dependencies—how compromise and recovery can move.

04

Uncertainty, provenance, and freshness

Distinguish declared, observed, inferred, and validated information. Preserve confidence, source, evidence, timestamps, and age.

05

Operational state over time

Preserve prior states for drift, incident timelines, exposure duration, changes in trust, configurations, and earlier decisions.

06

The standard for current state

Reconcile missing, stale, conflicting, duplicated, or weak observations. Operational state is estimated from evidence; it is not simply known.

A current-state model is useful only when its uncertainty remains visible.

Phase 03 · 07–12

Reasoning and operational use

What does this imply, what should change, and is it helping?

07

Expectations used for comparison

Select current architectures, patterns, trust requirements, policies, threat models, intended flows, and obligations.

08

AI explanations and hypotheses

Bound summaries, deviations, attack paths, competing explanations, investigation priorities, consequence estimates, and suggested action. Require evidence and falsification criteria.

09

Authority to influence observation

Decide whether conclusions may change logging, telemetry, sensors, detections, investigations, thresholds, or configuration—and control visibility risk and cost.

10

Independent validation

Use investigations, authoritative changes, controlled tests, human review, independent samples, incident outcomes, or trusted records. The model must not validate itself.

11

Change scope, depth, and focus

Adjust aperture for incidents, audits, reviews, recovery, inquiries, and briefings while remaining traceable and reproducible.

12

Trustworthiness and usefulness

Measure coverage, accuracy, freshness, relationship quality, calibration, false inference, validation, usefulness, review burden, cost, decision time, and reduced uncertainty. Output volume does not establish value.

04 / Compounding dependency

An early defect contaminates what follows.

Automation can increase the speed, reach, and apparent coherence of error.

Undefined scope
Incomplete discovery
Wrong relationships
Unsupported confidence
Inaccurate state
Weak hypotheses
Wrong observation
Biased validation
False assurance
An AI-enabled security system can become increasingly coherent, automated, and convincing while becoming progressively less connected to operational reality.

Readiness failure

Produces false coverage.

Structure failure

Produces false state.

Reasoning failure

Produces false confidence and misdirected action.

05 / Plan assessment

Compare the framework with your plan.

A credible plan makes boundaries, evidence, authority, validation, and economics explainable—not merely its tools.

A decision-maker’s test

If an answer is unclear, determine who must resolve it before the capability inherits trust.

Can we prove scope, exclusions, and accepted boundaries?

Does discovery reach every accountable zone?

Can we evidence each important relationship?

Can people distinguish declared, observed, inferred, and validated information?

Can we reconstruct prior operational state?

Can we explain uncertainty and conflicting evidence?

Can we justify the comparator?

Do we generate and falsify competing hypotheses?

Can AI influence observation without visibility loss or uncontrolled cost?

Is validation independent rather than self-confirming?

Are changes in aperture traceable and reproducible?

Do we measure accuracy, coverage, freshness, usefulness, cost, and decision impact?

What must be true before we are willing to rely on this capability—and what evidence will demonstrate that it remains true?

Cybersecurity foundations

Turn policy into protection—and prove it works.

A policy identifies what must happen. Assets have value. Risk describes what could go wrong. A security control reduces that risk, and detection and validation provide evidence that the control works as intended.

Policy requirementProtect the data service while allowing app-01 to reach db-01 only over TCP/443.

Your assignment at Northstar Services

A policy exists. Your job is to turn it into evidence.

Northstar Services operates an online customer portal. The application server, app-01, must retrieve customer records from db-01, a valuable data asset located in a more restricted security zone. If that data is exposed, changed, or unavailable, Northstar could lose customer trust, interrupt operations, and fail compliance obligations.

Management has approved a firewall policy: app-01 may connect to db-01 using encrypted HTTPS traffic on TCP port 443, and unrelated inbound services must remain blocked. This administrative control defines the requirement. You are the junior cybersecurity analyst responsible for implementing a technical firewall control and collecting evidence that it protects the assets without interrupting the authorized business process.

You will see both sides of the CIA triad tradeoff: allowing TCP/443 supports availability, restricting other ports supports confidentiality, and limiting network paths reduces opportunities for unauthorized changes that could affect integrity.

What you will see

  1. A baseline test detects that the required business connection is unavailable.
  2. A precise firewall rule translates policy into a technical security control.
  3. The same test validates that authorized TCP/443 traffic now succeeds.
  4. A negative test confirms that unauthorized TCP/22 traffic remains blocked.
  5. Each result becomes evidence for governance, risk management, and compliance review.
